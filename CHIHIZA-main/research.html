O que é uma habitação rural sustentável?

What is a sustainable rural housing? Design of a prototype and conceptualization of the term

Author Felipe Emiliano Pérez Pinilla
Advisor Marcos Aurélio Saquet
University Universidade Estadual do Oeste do Paraná (UNIOESTE)
Program Master's in Sustainable Rural Development
Date March 2020
Pages 103

This thesis designed a sustainable rural housing prototype for communities in Tena, Cundinamarca, Colombia. The research combined bibliographic analysis, community surveys, and architectural design to create a housing model that integrates local materials (guadua bamboo, adobe, bahareque), climate-responsive design, and community participation.

The result: a replicable prototype where every material is sourced within 50km, passive design reduces energy needs by 60-80%, and community involvement from the design phase ensures long-term adoption.

This directly led to: CHIHIZA's 50km material sourcing policy. Every project begins with a local materials audit — what can be found, quarried, or grown within 50km of the site. This is not an ecological aspiration. It is a cost strategy validated by research.

Arquitectura sostenible y desarrollo rural

Sustainable architecture and rural development — Biomimetic design in the Colombian Andes

Author Felipe Emiliano Pérez Pinilla
University Universidad Distrital Francisco José de Caldas
Program Architecture

Undergraduate research on the intersection of sustainable architecture and rural development in Colombia. Explored how biomimetic design principles — structural curves inspired by seashells, ventilation modeled on termite mounds, load distribution borrowed from bone growth — can be applied to rural housing using local materials and traditional construction techniques.

This directly led to: CHIHIZA's approach to climate-responsive design. At Montvento, cross-ventilation was modeled on termite mound airflow principles. Roof overhangs were calculated using biomimetic solar geometry. The architecture does not fight the climate — it borrows from nature's solutions.

Modelo transversal de educación ambiental como estructura en el planteamiento de ciudad sostenible

Transversal model of environmental education as a structure for sustainable city planning — The case of Bavaria, Bogotá

Author María Fernanda Cortés Núñez
University Universidad Agustiniana
Program Architecture
Date 2018

This thesis addressed the urban renewal of the Bavaria factory site in Kennedy, Bogotá — 46 sectors in Castilla and 113 in Bavaria. The research proposed an environmental education model integrated into urban planning, connecting ecological corridors, citizen participation, and the Main Ecological Structure (EPP) to transform industrial land into sustainable urban space.

The key insight: sustainable cities are not built through infrastructure alone, but through the environmental education of their communities. Architecture must teach as much as it shelters.

This principle directly informs CHIHIZA's approach to community development — every project includes cultural integration and environmental literacy as core deliverables.

This directly led to: CHIHIZA's community integration framework. Every development includes environmental literacy programs, local workforce training, and cultural integration protocols. We do not just build houses — we help build informed communities.

Análisis de precios unitarios de construcciones tradicionales en tapia pisada

Unit price analysis of traditional rammed earth construction — Boyacá and Santander, Colombia

Author Julián David Pérez Pinilla
Advisor Oscar Torres, Hernando Acuña
University Universidad Distrital Francisco José de Caldas
Program Specialization in Property Appraisal
Date 2020

Traditional rammed earth (tapia pisada) construction has been gradually displaced by industrialized materials, yet it persists in rural Colombia. This research determined the commercial value per square meter of rammed earth buildings in municipalities of Boyacá and Santander — providing the first updated pricing framework for this construction system.

The findings are critical for CHIHIZA: traditional earth construction can be economically competitive with conventional systems when properly valued. This research provides the financial evidence to justify local material sourcing across all CHIHIZA projects.

This directly led to: CHIHIZA's financial viability framework. When we tell investors that local materials are cost-competitive, we have the data to prove it. Julián David's pricing research eliminates the guesswork from material budgeting.

Centro de apoyo para los programas de Resocialización

Support center for resocialization programs — Case study: La Modelo Prison, Bogotá

Authors Diana Carolina Guantiva Ospina, Julián David Pérez Pinilla
University Universidad Agustiniana
Program Architecture
Date 2018

The absence of spaces for resocialization is one of the main causes of recidivism and prison overcrowding. This research proposed an architectural solution: adapting spaces for inmate training restores not only quality of life but the right to social reintegration.

The study of La Modelo Prison in Bogotá demonstrated that architecture can be a tool for social transformation — not just through buildings, but through landscapes, gardens, and environments that restore human dignity.

This philosophy extends to CHIHIZA: architecture is not just about structures. It is about the lives that unfold within them. Every project considers how space shapes behavior, community, and wellbeing.

This directly led to: CHIHIZA's human-centered design philosophy. Every project asks: how will this space shape the lives of the people who inhabit it? Not just functionally — but emotionally, socially, and psychologically. Architecture must serve human dignity.

Montvento

A house designed to breathe. Bucaramanga, Colombia.

The Beginning

Not every project begins with a blueprint.

Some begin with a landscape.

Montvento is born in the mountains of Bucaramanga, where architecture does not seek to dominate the territory, but to become a natural extension of it.

Here, the terrain was the first architect.

The slope.
The wind.
The orientation of the sun.
The existing vegetation.
The distant views.

Every decision was a response to the place.

This was not simply about building a house.

It was about building a different way of living.

The Problem

The site presented real challenges.

A steep slope running north to south. Intense afternoon sun from the west. Strong seasonal winds funneled through the valley. Limited road access for heavy materials. Soil conditions that demanded careful foundation design.

Most architects would flatten the site and build what they wanted. We chose to listen instead.

The slope became the organization principle � the house steps with the terrain, not against it. The western sun became an opportunity for deep overhangs that cool the interior naturally. The wind informed the cross-ventilation strategy that eliminates the need for air conditioning.

Every constraint became a design driver.

Architecture

From the very first approach, the project was conceived as an open architecture.

A house where the boundary between interior and exterior practically disappears.

The large social spaces extend toward the terraces. The roof overhangs generate deep shade. The corridors function as climatic transition spaces.

Nature is never excluded from the architecture.

It is always present.

The Program

With approximately 300 square meters of built area, Montvento organizes its program around a clear and tranquil sequence.

Five bedrooms. Multiple bathrooms. Open social spaces. Integrated kitchen. Pool. Parking.

But beyond the functional layout, every room was designed to privilege a specific experience.

Materials

The materials were chosen for their honesty.

Stone connects the house to the terrain.

Wood adds warmth and ages with dignity.

The ceramic tile roof overhangs maintain a deeply regional identity.

The light finishes reflect natural light and allow the landscape to be the true protagonist.

There are no materials that try to call attention to themselves.

Each one serves a function.
Each one belongs to the place.

Design Decisions

Every design decision at Montvento was a direct response to the site analysis.

Orientation: The main living spaces face north to capture consistent natural light without the harsh western sun. Bedrooms face east for gentle morning light.

Roof overhangs: Calculated at 1.2 meters to block summer sun while allowing winter light � reducing cooling loads by an estimated 40%.

Cross ventilation: Every room has openings on two sides. The valley breeze does the work that air conditioning would otherwise require.

Material sourcing: Stone quarried within 20km. Wood from managed local forests. Ceramic tiles from regional manufacturers. Every material has a story rooted in this territory.

Stepped foundation: Rather than cutting the hillside, the foundation follows the natural contour � reducing excavation, preserving drainage patterns, and creating a house that feels like it grew from the ground.

Experience

Architecture is also something you feel.

And at Montvento, that experience occurs through small decisions.

The corridors protected from the sun. The permanent shade. The cross ventilation. The sound of the wind passing through the roof. The texture of stone underfoot. The wood slowly warming in the afternoon light.

Here, architecture is not merely observed.

It is inhabited with all the senses.

Landscape

One of the fundamental principles of the project was to allow the landscape to constantly enter the house.

Every opening was placed to capture a specific view. Every window frames a different portion of the territory.

The mountains appear as a permanent element of the architectural composition.

They are not the backdrop of the project.
They are part of the project.

Life

Everyday life changes when the landscape participates in it.

Breakfast looking at the mountains. Working accompanied by natural light. Resting under a roof open to the wind. Sharing with family in spaces that never feel enclosed.

The architecture slowly disappears to make space for the experience.

What We Learned

Every project teaches us something. Montvento was no exception.

The site always knows better. The more we listened to the terrain, the better the design became. The moments we almost imposed our will were the moments we had to step back and reconsider.

Local materials are not a compromise. Stone from 20km away performed better, cost less, and looked more authentic than any imported alternative. Juli�n David's research on traditional construction economics proved itself in practice.

Passive design works. Cross ventilation and strategic overhangs reduced energy needs dramatically. The house stays comfortable without mechanical cooling � a direct result of Emiliano's research on climate-responsive rural housing.

Constraints produce creativity. The steep slope, the limited access, the challenging soil � these were not obstacles. They were the ingredients of a better design.

Montvento is not just a house. It is proof that the CHIHIZA methodology works.
